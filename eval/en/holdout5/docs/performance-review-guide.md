# Performance review guide for managers

Performance reviews is held in January and February. The review focuses on how the employee can grow, not on what they have done wrong. The guidance of the HR team is summarised here; the full policy is on the intranet.

## Preparation

The manager read what the employee has written in the self-assessment. The self-assessment is filled in Populus no later than a week before the review. Employees who have not filled it in gets a reminder; the number of reminders sent is tracked.

Book an hour and a quiet room. Trust that the employee can assess their own work; the review is not an interrogation but a conversation. The quality of the conversation matters more then the form.

## Structure

1. A look back at the year. What worked, what did not, and why.
2. Goals for next year. The goals are agreed together, not dictated.
3. Skills and training. The kind of training offered depends on where the employee wants to grow.
4. Wellbeing. Ask directly whether the employee is coping.

The manager's job is to listen more than to talk. Employees notices immediately if the manager have already decided the outcome. The notes are entered in Populus within a week, which are also the deadline for the employee's comments; the comments of the employee are optional.

## Setting goals

The goals build on the unit's goals, but they is not just numbers. A good goal say what the employee will do differently. Focus on what the employee can influence; do not tie the goal to what others do. A vague goal is worse than no goal.

There are at most four goals. More goals scatters attention, which has been shown year after year. Goals that are not measured are forgotten, and the ones that are measured badly steer badly. Each of the four goals has an owner and a date.

## Difficult conversations

If the employee's performance has declined, say so directly but respectfully. Be aware that the cause may lie outside work. Do not promise what you cannot keep; agree on a follow-up in a month. A difficult conversation does not get easier by being postponed; the group of managers who postpone regrets it.

The employee may ask for the shop steward to be present. The manager treats that neutrally; it says nothing about the employee distrusting the manager. HR help if the conversation stalls, and they have offered coaching for new managers such as Lauri Nieminen and Ananya Iyer.
