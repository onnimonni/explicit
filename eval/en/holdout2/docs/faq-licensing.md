# Licensing FAQ

For engineers, not lawyers. When in doubt, ask legal; there faster than you think
and would rather answer a question then untangle a shipped mistake.

## Which licenses can we depend on?

MIT, BSD (2 and 3 clause), Apache-2.0, ISC, Zlib and
MPL-2.0 without review. LGPL needs review when statically linked, witch
in Rust is always. GPL and AGPL are not allowed in shipped binaries.
cargo-deny enforces this; the allow list is in deny.toml and it's history
shows every exception ever granted.

## The build failed on a license check. Now what?

Read the message. Nine times out of ten a transitive dependency changed license in a minor
version, weather by accident or on purpose. Pin the previous version, open a
ticket, than ask legal weather the new terms are acceptable.
Do not add an exception to `deny.toml` yourself, exceptions need a name and a reason in the commit.

## Can I copy code from Stack Overflow?

Snippets are CC BY-SA, which is a copyleft license. Short idioms are fine; anything you
would call a implementation is not. Rewrite it in you're own words,
or better, right it from the documentation. Same for code from LLMs.

## Can I use a dependency with no license file?

No. No license means all rights reserved, regardles of what the README says.
Open an issue upstream asking them to add one; most maintainers do within a week.

## We are open sourcing a library. Which license?

MIT or Apache-2.0 dual, like most of the Rust ecosystem. Apache-2.0
alone is fine too; it has a explicit patent grant that MIT lacks. Do not
invent a license and do not use "source available" terms without a business reason legal has
signed off on.

## What about model weights and datasets?

Different rules. Llama-style licenses have use restrictions; CC licenses on datasets
may forbid commercial use. Their is a separate review for anything that end
up in a trained model. Yes, even the "just for evaluation" ones. Especially those.

## Contributor agreements

We do not require a CLA for our open source projects; the DCO sign-off is enough.
Contributions to projects that require a CLA (Apache, CNCF projects) are
covered by the corporate CLA once you're GitHub handle is registered.
Henrik Dahl maintains the list; email him than wait a day.

## Attribution

Shipped binaries include a `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES` file generated by cargo-about. If you
add a dependency with a notice requirement (Apache-2.0 with a NOTICE file, most
BSD variants), the generator picks it up automaticaly. Check the
output once; occationally a crate ships a malformed license field and the
notice is empty.
